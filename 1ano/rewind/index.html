PRESENT CONTINUOUS

Forma: verbo BE + verbo principal + -ing

+−?
I am watchingI'm not watchingAm I watching?
She is postingShe isn't postingIs she posting?
They are playingThey aren't playingAre they playing?

Uso: ação AGORA (now, right now, at this moment) ou em andamento nestes dias.

Spelling: come→coming · run→running · lie→lying · sit→sitting

Regra rápida: depois de verbo ou preposição (to, with, for, at) → pronome objeto.
She is calling him, but he is ignoring her.

Usa-se quando sujeito e objeto são a mesma pessoa.
by + reflexive = "sozinho": He did it by himself.
Ênfase: The singer herself answered my comment.

LINKING WORDS · SO / YET

SO = consequência ("então", "por isso")
I have tests this week, so I'm not going out.

YET = contraste ("no entanto", "mesmo assim")
I have tests this week. Yet, I'm not studying.

Truque: se a segunda parte é o resultado esperado → SO.
Se é o oposto do esperado → YET.

👎
"I'm tired, yet I'm sleeping." ❌ (faz sentido nenhum)
👍
"I'm tired, so I'm sleeping." ✅

ROUND 3 · READING COMPREHENSION

📖 TEXT 1 — Are you using AI, or is AI using you?

Are you using AI, or is AI using you?

Every student knows the feeling. The essay is due tomorrow, the document is still empty, and your brain suddenly decides that reorganizing your entire bedroom is urgent. So you open a chatbot, type three words, and forty seconds later the essay is done. Problem solved — or is it? Right now, half of this class is probably thinking about a task they are avoiding.

Researchers who study learning are not against these tools. They worry about one thing only: the difference between using AI to think and using it so you don't have to. A student who asks an AI to explain the difference between its and it's is learning something. A student who asks it to write the whole paragraph is outsourcing the one thing school is actually for.

So here is the honest question. When you open that app tonight, what are you really asking it to do?

due: com prazo para · essay: redação · to outsource: terceirizar, passar para outro

CONSUMERISM & POSSESSIVE PRONOUNS

Chapter 5 — saver or spender? E a diferença que derruba mais gente na prova.

THE ONE RULE
possessive ADJECTIVEpossessive PRONOUN
Imy phonemine
youyour bikeyours
hehis consolehis
sheher watchhers
itits battery—
weour carours
theytheir laptoptheirs

Adjective vem antes de um substantivo. Pronoun vem sozinho, no lugar do substantivo.
My car is old and your car is new. → Mine is old and yours is new.

⚠️ Nunca existe apóstrofo: yours, hers, ours, theirs — nunca "your's".

ROUND 4 · READING COMPREHENSION · ENEM STYLE

📖 TEXT 2 — How many subscriptions are you paying for?

How many subscriptions are you paying for?

Ask a teenager how much they spend on streaming and the answer is almost always "nothing — it's my parents' account". Ask the parents, and the number changes completely. One video platform, one music app, one cloud for the photos, one game pass: separately, each one looks cheap. Together, they cost more than the sneakers everybody wants.

Companies know this. They design the price so that it feels invisible: small, monthly, automatic. You don't decide to pay it every month — you simply forget to cancel it. That is not an accident; it is the product.

The conscious consumer question from Chapter 5 still works here. Before your next purchase, ask yourself: do I really want this, or do I just want to want it?

subscription: assinatura · cheap: barato · purchase: compra · to cancel: cancelar
